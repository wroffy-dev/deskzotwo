# Resellers first, shops later: re-ranking Deskzo's verticals

The "Deskzo Multi-Industry SaaS Opportunity & Double-Validation Report" (30 Oct 2026) gets its posture right and several of its numbers wrong. Its core call is sound for Deskzo: don't build 30 vertical apps at once, run paid pilots with kill criteria, and treat GS1 and TecDoc catalog rights and Microsoft API access as things to prove before building. Its anchor facts hold up only partly. The ₹5 crore e-invoicing threshold is still the law. The "99.9 million Udyam + UAP registrations" could not be confirmed and sits at the very top of what is plausible; the latest figure that could be confirmed is about 8.9 crore (89 million) in July 2026, and 99.3% of registrants are micro firms. The report also misses the October 2025 Microsoft rule that takes the Partner Center API away from almost every small reseller, so its "provisioning" wedge has to run through distributors instead. Its weakest part is the economics. It models ₹399–799 per business per month on a freemium funnel, but Deskzo sells Deskzo One at ₹1,499 per person per month with a trial and no free plan, so the scenario table describes a different company. Checked against competitor prices, the plan's service pilots (salon, garage, packers & movers, kirana, milk delivery) look worse than scored. Indian tools in those trades charge per shop, often ₹230–4,000 a month for the whole business, and Fresha's core product is free. The revised recommendation keeps the CONDITIONAL GO but narrows it. Lead with Microsoft/Autodesk software resellers using Wroffy as the design partner, follow with small MSPs and IT-hardware distributors, demote FMCG distribution, and keep shop-floor verticals parked unless Deskzo creates a per-location edition.

## What the source report gets right

The report's discipline is its best feature, and it is unusual for a strategy memo. It grades evidence (A, B, C, U). It marks willingness to pay, free-to-paid conversion, churn, CAC and gross margin as **not validated**. It sets pass and fail thresholds before each experiment. It also says "Adjust only before running each experiment, not after seeing outcomes", which guards against moving the bar after seeing results. Its caution on catalogs matches what the research found. GS1 India's DataKart page for solution providers describes a paid, KYC-gated registration (PAN, GST, ROC certificate, two years of audited balance sheets, proof of fee payment). The page publishes no API, field list, fee amounts or redistribution terms ([GS1 India](https://www.gs1india.org/datakart/datakart-for-solution-providers)). The report is right that DataKart "is not automatically a perpetual free-to-redistribute national product database". It is also right that TecDoc licensing is a negotiation. TecAlliance's own terms grant only a "non-exclusive, non-transferable" right of use to data it owns ([TecAlliance GTC](https://a.storyblok.com/f/297549/x/d6dca87535/gtc-en.pdf)), and no public price list exists ([Dev-Opla](https://dev-opla.com/resources/what-is-tecdoc-auto-parts-ecommerce-guide)).

Its regulated list also holds up. Pharmacy, clinics, labs, and pharma and agri distribution belong later. The DPDP Rules were notified on 13 Nov 2025, and enforcement and penalties start on **13 Nov 2026**, two weeks from now (secondary source: [DEV/ecorpit](https://dev.to/mr_manushukla/abdm-hip-integration-in-2026-fhir-r4-abha-linking-and-nabh-6th-edition-readiness-50i4)). ABDM integration is becoming a practical expectation for health providers: NMC requires ABDM-compliant HMIS at medical colleges, and states tie AB-PMJAY empanelment to ABDM certification ([Omnicuris](https://www.omnicuris.com/medshots/editorial_picks/nmc-hmis-compliance-abdm-integration-steps_17-Jul-26_22-01)). Finally, the report puts software-licence workflows at the top (4.9), and that ranking survives every check below. The ranking was right even though the segment was mislabelled.

The Udyam correction matters less for the number itself than for what it implies. Even the confirmed 9 crore is overwhelmingly informal and micro. Udyam's own data-quality critics note that the register now exceeds the ASUSE estimate of 7.34 crore establishments, and that only 0.17% of registrants have ever deregistered ([IMPRI](https://www.impriindia.com/?p=76630)). The report already concedes that this is "not an addressable paying-market estimate". The useful pool for a ₹1,499-per-person product is the few lakh small and medium firms with office staff, not the crores of nano units.

The e-invoicing threshold points the same way. Deskzo already has NIC e-invoice and e-way bill encryption built in. That feature is a selling point only for businesses above ₹5 crore AATO, and the 30-day rule adds pressure above ₹10 crore. Software resellers and distributors clear those bars routinely. Salons, kirana stores and packers & movers mostly do not, so for them "GST books with e-invoicing" is a feature they will not pay for.

## Microsoft's US$1M rule turns "provisioning" into a distributor integration

The report's lead wedge is "License lifecycle + reseller portal + renewals" with "provisioning". It names the right risk ("CSP authorization, vendor API rights") but underestimates how decisive that risk has become. From **1 October 2025**, CSP direct-bill partners need at least **US$1M** of indirect CSP revenue at the global account level over the previous 12 months. They also need a Solutions Partner designation, a paid Advanced or Premier support plan, the security-score items and an automated capability assessment. Partners are reassessed every year, and those that fail drop to indirect reseller for at least a year ([MS Learn: direct-bill requirements](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/enroll/direct-partner-new-requirements)). The bar was US$300K in FY25. Distributors need at least US$30M per region, while indirect resellers need only about US$1,000 TTM ([TechPartner.News](https://www.techpartner.news/news/microsoft-announces-sweeping-csp-changes-adding-us1m-csp-revenue-threshold-to-direct-bill-616900)). Microsoft is explicit that "sandbox and Partner Center API/SDK capabilities are available only to direct-bill partners". Indirect resellers also "can't create Microsoft Entra tenants for their new customers" ([MS Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/enroll/transition-direct-to-indirect)).

For Deskzo, that settles the architecture. A Gurugram reseller the size of Wroffy, and almost every prospect like it, is an indirect reseller. Any provisioning or order automation has to go through the distributor. **Ingram Micro's Xvantage** publishes an OpenAPI spec and SDKs with endpoints for catalogue, orders, quotes, invoices and renewals ([GitHub](https://github.com/ingrammicro-xvantage/xi-sdk-openapispec)), and Ingram is pushing Xvantage hard in India ([CRN Asia](https://www.crnasia.com/india/news/2026/do-not-use-us-just-for-fulfilment-says-ingram-micro-as-it-pushes-xvantage-led-partner-growth)). **TD SYNNEX StreamOne Ion** has an OAuth API, but it is not confirmed in India ([API Evangelist](https://providers.apievangelist.com/providers/synnex/)). **Redington CloudQuarks**, which reportedly serves 40,000+ resellers and SMBs, has no public API ([Simply Wall St](https://simplywall.st/community/narratives/in/tech/nse-redington/redington-shares/redington-set-to-ride-the-ongoing-ai-and-cloud-boom-by-transforming-to-saas-marketplace-and-vendor-business-model)).

Several newer rules also belong in a renewal tool and are missing from the report. Partner-of-record validation now blocks changes to existing subscriptions from 1 Dec 2025 ([MS Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/customers/indirect-provider-tasks-in-partner-center)). Two FY27 changes are reported by Pax8 and Work365 but not yet checked on a Microsoft page: a **5% uplift on annual-term software subscriptions billed monthly** from 1 Oct 2026, and usage-based Copilot Business billing from 1 Dec 2026 ([Work365](https://resources.work365apps.com/blog/october-2026-csp-software-price-change); [Pax8](https://www.pax8.com/blog/?p=6908)).

Autodesk, Wroffy's other line, changes the picture just as much, and the report never mentions it. Under Autodesk's "new transaction model", the partner quotes and Autodesk transacts and collects directly from the customer. The partner's earnings become an Autodesk-paid incentive rather than a resale margin ([Autodesk Q3 FY24](https://investors.autodesk.com/static-files/bad7ff29-5489-4da9-86fd-7b29caba2341)). Autodesk said Solution Providers worldwide primarily serve customers under this model by FY26 Q4 ([Q4 FY26 call](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/02/26/autodesk-adsk-q4-2026-earnings-call-transcript/)). In June 2026 it also revamped its self-serve India Store for SMBs ([YourStory](https://yourstory.com/enterprise-story/2026/06/autodesk-revamps-india-store-targets-startups-smbs-self-serve-digital-platform)). Whether India has gone live on the agency model is unconfirmed. If it has, Deskzo's Autodesk renewal flow should track quotes, incentives and renewal opportunities rather than raise GST invoices. Wroffy can answer this from its partner portal in minutes.

The correction to the report's labels follows from this. The 4.9-scored segment is **software and cloud licence resellers**, not distributors. Distributors at US$30M+ run their own marketplaces. Resellers are the ones running renewal calendars, quotes, GST invoices, collections and OEM rebates on "CRM + Tally + distributor portal + Excel". That last point is an inference from the absence of any India-specific tool in the research. The CSP tools that do exist (Work 365, Datto's Goolash, CSP Control Centre) are built around Autotask and ConnectWise, not Tally or GST ([Datto](https://www.datto.com/integrations/goolash-1/); [Work 365](https://azuremarketplace.microsoft.com/en/marketplace/apps/iotapapps.e1ae1652-82c2-4f8b-b846-d5d59d0be038)). Deskzo already ships most of that gap, including orders, subscriptions and renewals, the reseller channel with end customers, e-invoicing and OEM rebates, and it has a live reference customer.

The pattern is consistent. Every service and retail tool Indian shop owners already use is priced **per business or per location**, at about ₹230 a month (ePackBook) to ₹4,000 a month (Dingg). Commentators on Indian SMB buying describe the same preference for flat yearly pricing over seat-based pricing ([EmbedWorkflow](https://embedworkflow.com/blog/selling-saas-in-india/)). At ₹1,499 per person, a five-chair salon pays about ₹7,500 a month against a free Fresha core or a ₹2,000–4,000 Dingg. A six-person garage pays about ₹9,000 against ₹2,400–3,000 starter plans. A packers & movers firm pays dozens of times ePackBook's whole-business price. The report scored salon 4.5, garage 4.4 and packers & movers 4.3. Willingness to pay was supposed to carry 20% of each score, but those numbers do not reflect these price anchors. On per-person pricing, packers & movers is the most price-resistant vertical on the list.

FMCG distribution, at 4.8, is squeezed from both sides. Brands pay Bizom and FieldAssist to put DMS and SFA into their distributors, and the distributor keeps its books on cheap perpetual Tally, Marg or BUSY ([Marg pricing](https://itforsme.in/pricing/marg-erp-india)). That leaves little reason to buy a third, per-person system. The opening that remains, multi-brand distributors wanting one view across brands, is a niche.

The professional segments look the opposite. MSP tools are priced in dollars and pounds at roughly US$66–250 per technician, which is several times Deskzo's price before any rupee conversion. SuperOps' move to a 150-endpoint minimum leaves the 2–10-technician Indian IT shop underserved ([SuperOps](https://superops.com/pricing); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/30/superops-bags-25m-to-use-ai-and-better-help-managed-service-providers)). Deskzo has no RMM, so the fair comparison is the PSA half: tickets, AMC contracts, renewals and GST billing. On that half, ₹1,499 is cheap. Against all-in-one suites, Deskzo One sits almost exactly at Zoho One All-Employee (about ₹1,500, but every payroll employee must be licensed). It is well under Zoho One Flexible (about ₹3,500–4,000), above Odoo Custom (₹1,080–1,690 after a Sept 2026 rise), below Salesforce Starter (about ₹2,000), and far below Business Central Essentials (₹5,500+) ([itforsme](https://itforsme.in/pricing/zoho-one-india); [oec.sh](https://oec.sh/blog/odoo-price-hike-2026-09); [Salesforce IN](https://www.salesforce.com/in/sales/pricing/); [Cloudfy](https://cloudfysystems.com/blog/microsoft-dynamics-365-pricing-india)). The Zoho and Odoo figures come from partner sites; the vendor pages could not be fetched. Deskzo's price is defensible exactly where the buyer is an office-led B2B firm comparing it with Zoho One.

## The ₹399–799 model describes a company Deskzo is not

On their own terms, the report's assumptions are reasonable. The ₹399–799 per business per month sits squarely within Indian SMB list prices: myBillBook at ₹291–570, TallyPrime Silver at ₹675–750, Zoho Books Standard at about ₹749–899 ([Tally](https://tallysolutions.com/buy-tally/); [Patron Accounting](https://www.patronaccounting.com/blog/zoho-books-pricing-india-2026)). A 3–10% conversion fits freemium benchmarks of roughly 2.6–5% median, 8–12% top quartile, and ChartMogul's 8% cross-model median ([ChartMogul](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-conversion-report/); [Flint](https://www.flint.com/blog/b2b-saas-free-trial-conversion-rate-statistics)). The arithmetic also checks out: 700 × ₹599 = ₹4,19,300, and a 20% channel discount gives ₹3,35,440.

None of that is Deskzo's model. Deskzo One is ₹1,499 per person per month before GST, a year costs ten months (about ₹1,249 a month on annual terms), and there is a trial and **no free plan**. So the relevant benchmark is free-trial conversion: about 8% median in Kyle Poyar's 200-product sample, about 18% for opt-in trials, and about 49% when a card is required ([Userpilot](https://userpilot.com/blog/free-trial-conversion-rate/)). Freemium is the wrong benchmark. The unit is also a person, not a business, so ARPA scales with headcount. A 10-seat reseller pays about ₹15,000 a month, 19–38 times the report's ARPA.

The result changes the plan's scale. **Twenty paying reseller workspaces at ten seats each produce about ₹3.0 lakh MRR at list price** (20 × 10 × ₹1,499 = ₹2,99,800), or about ₹2.5 lakh on annual terms. That is within reach of the report's "base illustration", which needed 10,000 active businesses and 700 paying ones. The two paths imply very different companies: a sales-assisted B2B suite for a few hundred firms with office staff, versus a self-serve funnel for tens of thousands of micro shops. Deskzo's build — reseller channel, GST books with e-invoicing, payroll, helpdesk with portal — fits the first.

Churn sharpens the contrast. Global SMB SaaS logo churn runs 3–7% a month ([Vena](https://www.venasolutions.com/blog/saas-churn-rate)), and there is no Indian primary benchmark. At 5% a month, a ₹599 account yields about ₹12,000 of lifetime revenue before costs. That caps acquisition spend at a few thousand rupees and rules out field sales or hand-held onboarding, both of which kirana and salon pilots would need. A ₹15,000-a-month reseller account at the same churn yields about ₹3 lakh, which pays for a demo, data migration from Tally or Zoho, and a distributor connector. Deskzo's built migration tools (Zoho pull, opening balances, CRM and HRMS imports) are the kind of onboarding that only high-ARPA accounts can fund.

### Per-person versus per-business: a pricing decision the pilots force

Per-person pricing should stay the default for office-led firms (resellers, MSPs, B2B distributors' back offices). There it beats Zoho One by not forcing a licence for every employee. It cannot win any shop-floor vertical as it stands. If Deskzo pursues those verticals later, it needs one of two structural changes, and the research supports either. The first is a **per-location edition** priced against the ₹2,000–4,000 salon and ₹2,400–3,000 garage anchors, with staff included. The second is a **cheap staff or field seat** in the ₹99–299 range (Deskzo People at ₹99 is the precedent) for technicians and stylists who only need a job view, alongside full-price office seats. Fresha's ₹250–279 per-member add-ons and Zoho FSM's appointment-based model show the market already prices frontline staff that low. Both options need the per-product licensing fix already on Deskzo's list: today users are not tied to a product, so cheap seats would leak access to the full suite.

### UAE: a dated buying window, not a 2026 pilot

The UAE facts the report left out are concrete. Mandatory Peppol-based B2B and B2G e-invoicing through Accredited Service Providers starts on **1 Jan 2027** for businesses with revenue of AED 50m or more. Those businesses must appoint an ASP by **30 Oct 2026**, which is today. Businesses under AED 50m go live on **1 Jul 2027** and must appoint an ASP by **31 Mar 2027** ([KPMG](https://kpmg.com/us/en/taxnewsflash/news/2026/07/tnf-uae-phased-implementation-of-e-invoicing-beginning-january-1-2027.html)). The penalty is reported as AED 5,000 a month ([Tally MENA](https://tallysolutions.com/mena/uae-vat/uae-e-invoicing-timeline-2026-2027-complete-phase-by-phase-implementation-guide/)). The UAE had about 1.4 million companies at the end of 2025, and SMEs are about 95% of businesses ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/economy/2026/01/06/uae-adds-250000-more-companies-in-2025-as-llc-rules-simplified); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/business/markets/uae-small-business-licences-jump-over-900-in-two-decades-1.500664805)). Deskzo's NIC e-invoice integration does not carry over to Peppol. The SME window closes in March 2027, before an India reseller pilot could prove itself, so the UAE stays a watch item. The cheapest step is to find out whether any Indian reseller customers have UAE entities and which ASP they appoint.

## Conclusion

The source report treats Deskzo as a blank-slate horizontal platform looking for verticals, so it ranks industries by how large and painful they look. Deskzo is not a blank slate. It is a per-person B2B suite already running a Microsoft and Autodesk reseller. That turns the question around: which buyers already pay per person for office software, sit above the e-invoicing threshold, and lack a GST-native tool? Software resellers and small MSPs match all three. The salons, garages and kirana stores that drove most of the report's pilot list match none of them under current pricing.

The most consequential correction is Microsoft's October 2025 rule, more than any market number. It turns "provisioning" from a Partner Center feature into a distributor-integration business. Deskzo can turn that into a moat, because reseller tools built for Autotask do not reconcile Ingram or Redington data into GST books. The plan stays a CONDITIONAL GO. The condition is now three paying resellers and a working distributor connector within 90 days, not 10,000 free sign-ups.
